# Reward System — Engagement and Gamification Platform

**Role:** Product Manager  
**Date:** 2025 (as shown in portfolio source)  
**Focus:** Engagement and retention  
**Surfaces:** Educator dashboard and global leaderboard  
**Status:** Shipped (as shown in portfolio source)

## 1. Product overview

The Reward System is a gamified engagement layer designed to encourage adoption and sustained use of platform features. It recognizes meaningful user actions through points, badges, milestones, progression and leaderboards.

The product idea was to make progress visible and give users recognition for activities that represent meaningful engagement with the platform.

## 2. The problem

The source portfolio identifies a core product challenge: users needed stronger motivation to discover and consistently use other platform features.

Simply making features available does not guarantee that users will understand their value or build a habit around using them. The product needed a mechanism that could recognize progress and encourage users to explore more of the platform.

**Product problem statement:** How might we encourage users to adopt valuable platform features and sustain meaningful usage by making progress and achievement visible?

## 4. Product decisions and rationale

The documented product decisions included:

- **Points and badges:** provide visible recognition for milestones and achievements.
- **Progression tiers:** give users a sense of progression as they complete meaningful actions.
- **Educator dashboard:** give each user a personal view of progress, achievements and unlocked badges.
- **Global leaderboard:** introduce a platform-wide view of top performers.
- **Defined reward logic and triggers:** make the mechanics explicit and implementable with engineering.

### Trade-offs to explain in an interview

These are relevant product tensions implied by the solution. Confirm which were actively debated and how they were resolved:

- **Motivation versus manipulation:** rewards can encourage discovery, but excessive or poorly designed incentives can encourage users to chase points instead of getting value from the product.
- **Competition versus inclusion:** leaderboards can motivate some users but discourage others, especially users who are new or have fewer opportunities to earn points.
- **Simplicity versus sophistication:** a small set of understandable milestones is easier to learn and maintain than a complicated points economy.
- **Recognition versus gaming:** reward triggers must recognize meaningful actions and be designed to reduce repetitive or artificial activity.
- **Individual progress versus global comparison:** a personal dashboard supports self-improvement, while a leaderboard adds social comparison. Both serve different motivations.

## 6. Outcomes and impact

**Delivered:** a gamification layer with points, badges, progression and educator-facing and global leaderboard experiences. The portfolio describes the product as shipped and refers to thousands of users on the global leaderboard.

**Intended value:** encourage feature discovery, reinforce meaningful usage habits, recognize user achievements and strengthen ongoing engagement.

**What is not yet quantified:** the source does not provide a verified adoption baseline, post-launch uplift, retention comparison or evidence that the reward system caused a change in usage. The reference to thousands of leaderboard users should not be presented as proof of incremental adoption.

### Metrics I would use to evaluate the product

- Reward-system activation rate.
- Percentage of eligible users completing their first milestone.
- Feature adoption among users exposed to reward prompts versus a comparable group.
- Repeat completion of meaningful actions over time.
- Progression from first reward to subsequent milestones.
- 30-, 60- and 90-day retention for eligible users.
- Distribution of points and badges to detect over-rewarding or gaming.
- User feedback on clarity, fairness and motivation.

Where possible, an experiment or phased rollout would help separate the effect of the reward system from other changes in the product.
