# Nexus — Internal Task and Project Management Platform

**Status:** Shipped (as shown in portfolio source)

## 1. Product overview

Nexus is an internal task and project management platform designed to bring project boards, sprint planning, support tickets and software development tracking into one workspace.

The product addressed a coordination problem: teams needed a shared view of work across planning, execution and support rather than having delivery activities fragmented across separate processes and tools.

## 2. The problem

The underlying problem was not simply the absence of another task tracker. It was the lack of a unified operational picture across cross-functional product delivery.

When project work, sprint activity and support requests are managed separately, teams can struggle to understand priorities, dependencies, ownership and progress. This creates context switching and makes it harder for product, design and engineering to stay aligned.

**Product problem statement:** How might we give product, design and engineering teams a shared workspace to plan delivery, manage support work and track projects from one place?

## 3. Discovery and problem framing

The following is therefore a transparent reconstruction of the product framing, not a claim that a particular research study was conducted.

The discovery focus can be explained through four areas:

- **Workflow understanding:** identify how teams currently organize projects, plan sprints, raise or triage support tickets and communicate progress.
- **Pain-point identification:** understand where fragmented workflows create duplicated effort, poor visibility or unnecessary context switching.
- **User and stakeholder needs:** distinguish what product managers, engineers and other team members need to see and do in a shared workspace.
- **Scope definition:** determine which connected workflows are essential for an initial useful release.

The central insight was that project tracking, sprint planning and support management were related parts of the same delivery operation. Bringing them together could improve shared visibility.

## 4. Product decisions and rationale

The portfolio describes the following product scope and decisions:

- **Bring core workflows into one workspace.** Project boards, sprint planning, support tickets and software development tracking were treated as connected capabilities rather than isolated tools.
- **Support team-level visibility.** Boards, users and analytics were included as key product surfaces.
- **Prioritize operational usefulness.** The product was designed around the activities teams needed to manage work, not just around displaying project status.
- **Work closely with engineering and QA.** Requirements and acceptance criteria were used to translate the product direction into deliverable functionality.

### Trade-offs to explain in an interview

These are the product tensions implied by the scope; confirm which ones were actual decisions you made before presenting them as historical facts:

- **Breadth versus depth:** bringing multiple workflows together creates value, but each workflow can become complex. A focused set of core capabilities is easier to deliver and adopt than attempting to replicate every feature of a mature project-management suite.
- **Centralization versus flexibility:** a shared workspace improves consistency, but teams may have different ways of working. The product needs enough structure for visibility without imposing unnecessary process.
- **Analytics versus actionability:** reporting is useful only when it helps teams make decisions. The most valuable metrics should connect to delivery and support workflows.
